Where zoning appeals actually happen.

Last checked: November 18, 2026

A zoning appeal sits at the crossing of three things: the statute that sets the deadline, the board that decides the case, and the housing market that supplies the projects. The links below cover all three — the state laws that fix the appeal windows, the research and board data that show how variances are really decided, the permit and shortage figures that size the demand, and the firms whose practices are built on this work.

Statutes: the deadlines

Massachusetts General Laws, Chapter 40A, Section 15 (Justia)

The text of the Massachusetts appeal statute: thirty days to appeal to the permit-granting authority, a hearing within sixty-five days, and a board decision within one hundred days.

Connecticut Office of Legislative Research — Time Period for Appealing Zoning Decisions (2004-R-0361)

Connecticut's legislative research office explains the two clocks: thirty days to appeal to the zoning board of appeals, and fifteen days to reach Superior Court from publication of the decision.

Town of Marshfield, MA — Board of Appeals Guide

A town's plain-English guide to its own board: thirty-day appeals, the twenty-day window to Superior Court after the decision is filed, and the board's decision deadlines.

Boards: how variances are decided

Pepperdine Law Review — Variances: A Canary in the Coal Mine for Zoning Reform?

John J. Infranca and Ronnie M. Farr's empirical study of variance decisions in Boston, Cambridge, Brookline, and Somerville, finding grants at rates often around ninety percent.

DC Local — DC's Zoning Code Makes Hundreds of People Ask Permission for Projects It Almost Never Rejects

An analysis of the District's Board of Zoning Adjustment case database — more than 14,000 cases, approval around 96 percent, and a median wait of about 99 days from filing to decision.

Jersey City — Annual Zoning Board of Adjustment Report, 2021

The board's own yearly report: 67 applications heard, 64 approved, a 96 percent rate, with 166 variances granted and the breakdown by variance type.

ZoneWire — Atlanta Zoning Hearings & Rezoning Votes

A running transcript analysis of Atlanta's land-use boards: 62 percent of 242 decisions approved over two years, with variances clearing only 52 percent.

ZoneWire — Tulsa Zoning & PUD Applications

The same transcript analysis for Tulsa: 85 percent of 440 decisions approved over two years, with variances at 66 percent and special exceptions at 87 percent.

Demand: permits, shortage, and sales

U.S. Census Bureau — 2025 Annual Building Permits

The Bureau's annual highlights: 1,431,616 new privately owned housing units authorized in 2025, down 3.1 percent from 2024, with a total valuation of $378.99 billion.

Align Lending — Housing Shortages: How They Push Prices Out of Reach

A guide that lines up the major shortage estimates against the Congressional Research Service's December 2025 review: Freddie Mac at 3.7 million, Zillow at 4.7 million, and a range of roughly 4 million to 5 million homes.

Scotsman Guide — Existing-home sales fall to 16-year low in October

Reporting on the National Association of Realtors' November 2026 release: sales at a 3.75 million annual rate, the slowest since August 2010, with the median price at $416,700.

Home Value Pros — Housing Supply Reform Adds 103K Homes to a 3.7M Hole

A report on Realtor.com's November 2026 coverage estimating that recently passed state housing laws could add roughly 103,000 homes a year against a shortfall near 3.7 million.

Greenberg Traurig — Land Use & Zoning

Greenberg Traurig's description of its national land-use practice, from acquisition and due diligence through entitlements, permitting, and construction, in markets from Miami to New York.

Holland & Knight — Land Use, DC and Northern Virginia

The firm's account of its National Capital Region land-use team, including variances, special exceptions, planned unit developments, and campus plans before local boards.
