The thesis

A name for the most urgent call in land-use law.

Zoning is the oldest form of land-use control in America — the Village of Euclid's ordinance was upheld by the Supreme Court in 1926 — and the zoning appeal is its pressure valve. When a permit is denied, a variance refused, or an enforcement order issued, the owner does not write a letter. The owner hires a lawyer, and hires one fast, because the statute gives the owner weeks, not months.

The deadlines are the practice. Massachusetts law gives thirty days to appeal a decision to the permit-granting authority, and twenty days after that to take the board's decision to Superior Court. Pennsylvania's Municipalities Planning Code gives thirty days. New Hampshire gives thirty days to reach the zoning board of appeals. Connecticut gives fifteen days to reach Superior Court from publication of the decision. Miss the window and the merits never get heard: the board loses jurisdiction, the court dismisses the appeal, and the denial stands. A firm that owns the words "zoning appeals" owns the search a property owner runs on the day the denial letter arrives.

What follows the appeal is a hearing, and the hearing is where the money is. Boards grant variances at rates that surprise outsiders — about nine in ten applications, according to the empirical study by John J. Infranca and Ronnie M. Farr in the Pepperdine Law Review — and the District of Columbia's Board of Zoning Adjustment approved around 96 percent of applications in its own case database, with a median wait of about 99 days from filing to decision. The work is high-stakes and recurring: every denied permit, every variance, every special exception is a separate matter, and every land-use firm in the country lists zoning appeals as a core service.

Exact, and built for a reachable buyer pool. The buyers are nameable: the thousands of land-use and zoning boutique firms in every state, the developers who appeal denials and seek variances on their own projects, and the legal lead-generation operators who sell the calls those owners make. They all search the same two words. This domain is those two words, in order, with nothing added.
